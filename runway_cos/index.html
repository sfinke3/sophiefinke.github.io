Built for the Chief of Staff role at Runway

A world model of the company

Runway's argument is that trial and error is cheap in simulation and expensive in the real world. Running a company works the same way. The expensive way to learn that an initiative stalled, or that a promise to a studio slipped, is to find out three weeks later. So instead of describing how I would do this job, I built the thing the role description asks for: a single source of truth where every top-priority initiative has a scope, an owner, and a timeline, every relationship is developed on a cadence instead of from memory, and every ask from the Co-CEO's conversations is tracked until it closes. Every figure is illustrative and built from public information. All figures illustrative

The idea in one line: Runway builds world models so mistakes can happen in simulation instead of in the real world. A Chief of Staff is that layer for a Co-CEO. Plans get pressure-tested against a live model of the company, and nothing has to quietly fail for three weeks to get noticed.
